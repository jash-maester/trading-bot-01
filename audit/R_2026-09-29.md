## E3 — a volatility-neutral training target

**Why.** E2 showed a listwise loss turned the model into a volatility bet
(+0.14 loading) and it lost. The MSE model leans the other way (−0.16), and
S3 showed low-vol is regime-dependent — it reversed on the holdout. S6 found
R4's information sits in the bottom tail, and the bottom tail of a
low-vol-tilted model is the high-vol names. Hypothesis: much of what R4
learns is the volatility factor, which crowds out stock-specific information
at the top. Removing volatility from what the model is asked to predict
should leave it only the stock-specific part.

**Variant (one).** Identical to `r4_pit_long` (config, windows, seed 42,
panel, pruned TCN, fp32 highest, MSE) except the **train-split targets** are
neutralised: per date, the cross-sectionally z-scored forward return is
regressed on the cross-sectional rank of `realized_vol_60d` among the
labelled names, and the model is trained on the re-z-scored residual.
Validation (early stopping) and test (the gates) stay on **raw** returns, so
the model is judged on what a book actually earns. Tag
`r4_pit_long_volneutral`.

**Pass (13 windows, all three)** — the same bar E2 faced:
- top-30 gate: paired t vs 20 random books > t_crit(95%) **and** rank ≤ 2 of 21;
- the rank-IC gate PASSES;
- top-30 mean net excess > `r4_pit_long`'s −0.00241 per 20d.

Also reported, not a criterion: the predictions' rank correlation with
`realized_vol_60d` (the manipulation check — it should be near 0).

**Only if it passes:** one look at the holdout top-30 gate.

## E4 — the E1 screen, re-formed quarterly

**Why.** E1 (bottom-30 screen, monthly) beat 20 random screens (rank 1/21,
paired t 2.43) but its absolute net gain (t 1.04) was eaten by turnover: the
random screens lost −0.00047 per 20d to costs alone. Re-forming every 60
sessions instead of 20 cuts that turnover roughly threefold.

**This is a second look at the screen hypothesis**, chosen after E1's result
was known. To keep the family-wise error at 5% over the two looks, E4 uses
**t_crit at 97.5% (two-sided), i.e. α/2**, not the usual 95%.

**Variant (one).** Screen out the bottom 30 by `r_hat_20d` (K=30 as in E1 and
S6), equal-weight the rest, re-form every **60** sessions, 60-day forward
return over the holding period, 23 bps round-trip cost on the changed
fraction, against 20 random screens of the same size, on `r4_pit_long`'s 13
windows.

**Pass (both):** window-level mean net excess over the universe > 0 with
t > t_crit(97.5%), **and** paired difference vs the random-screen mean > 0
with t > t_crit(97.5%).

### E4 — FAIL (2026-09-29 ~20:40 IST)

`scripts/topk_gate.py --signal r4_pit_long --which screen --k 30 --horizon 60
--score-horizon 20 --alpha 0.025` (`audit/topk_gate/r4_pit_long_screen30_quarterly.json`):

| | per 60d, net of cost | t | t_crit (97.5%) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| screen excess over the universe | **+0.00247** (10/13 windows > 0) | **2.14** | 2.56 | ✗ |
| paired vs 20 random screens | +0.00328, **rank 1 of 21** | 2.78 | 2.56 | ✓ |

Fails as pre-registered — and would fail at the ordinary 95% bar too
(2.14 < 2.18), so the stricter second-look threshold is not what decided it.
Quarterly re-forming did what was predicted: net gain ~0.5%/yr (E1) → ~1%/yr.
Across E1 and E4 the screen consistently beats random screens of the same
size (rank 1 of 21 both times), but its absolute benefit (~1%/yr) cannot be
distinguished from zero on 13 windows. **The screen idea is closed**: two
looks is the budget, and a third knob would be the search this project has
learned not to run. The `--score-horizon`/`--alpha` options default to the
old behaviour; a re-run of E1 in default mode reproduces its archived numbers
exactly.
